Voted Here, Counted There

A county's own roster lists someone as having voted there. The Secretary of State agrees that person voted — but files them under a different county. We take each county's voters that are missing from the state's list for that county, and find the ones the state did record, just in someone else's column.

Loading…

These are not lost voters — they are misplaced ones. The county says they voted in the county; the state says they voted, in a neighbor. The pattern is the tell: the leaks run between adjacent counties — the Dallas–Fort Worth counties into each other, the Rio Grande Valley into itself — which is what a genuine mis-filing looks like, not random noise. "Filed under" is the state's reported county, not where the voter is registered.

How to read this

  • On its list, not the state's is each county's own voters that the state's roster for that county does not carry — the same "county-only" figure as the main dashboard. It is mostly the state's list lagging a day behind, and for the big mail counties it is dominated by mail received but not yet posted.
  • State filed them elsewhere is the subset we can prove is not just lag: the state did record the vote — under another county's roster. That is the hard finding on this page.
  • Where the state put them lists the receiving counties and how many. Read Dallas's row as "the state filed N Dallas voters under Collin," and so on.
  • Not in a roster we hold is the remainder: either genuinely absent from the state's lists, or filed under one of the 240-odd counties we do not yet collect voter rosters for. We can't tell those two apart, so we don't claim to.
  • The limit, stated plainly: we can only see a voter land in another county when that county is one we collect (currently ). Leakage to counties outside that set is invisible to us, so the real cross-county total is at least what is shown here.
